# Reusable Jubensha Game Design Guidelines

This reference provides reusable game-design mechanics for any set of three theme items, any setting, and any player count (4–6).

---

## 1. Theme Integration Standards

All three input `theme_items` must be woven into the game's core design.

- **Structural Requirement**: At least **two** of the three items must perform a structural function in one or more of:
  1. **Central Mechanism / Incident**: How the incident occurred or was concealed.
  2. **Canonical Timeline**: How time, order of events, or alibis are established or manipulated.
  3. **Clue System**: A key physical/informational artifact players inspect, compare, or trade.
  4. **Motive / Core Conflict**: Why the responsible party acted or why factions collide.
  5. **Character Relationships / Secrets**: A hidden bond, obligation, or blackmail lever.
  6. **Final Solution**: An indispensable link in proving the central truth.
- **Third Item Requirement**: Even if the third item is not the primary mechanism, it must still create an active gameplay hook (e.g., a discoverable clue, a Round 2 escalation event, or a personal objective trigger).
- **Concrete Clue-Binding Rule**: Every single theme item MUST be instantiated in at least one numbered Clue (`C#`) or explicit player-facing mechanic — never mentioned only in the DM overview or abstractly in the theme mapping table.
- **Anti-Decoration Test**: Ask, *"If I deleted this theme item from the clues and booklets, would a deduction step, alibi, motive, or ending branch break?"* If no, redesign its role before Stage 2.

## 2. Core Mechanics That Make a Jubensha Fun

1. **Asymmetric Information & Forced Interaction**
   - No player starts with the full picture.
   - Give every player at least **one piece of private information** that another player needs, and at least **one question** only another player can answer.
   - Design at least **two cross-player information dependencies** (e.g., P1 holds a partial timestamp; P4 holds the matching witness sighting).

2. **Distributed Suspicion & Active Play for Everyone**
   - At least **3 of the 4–6 players** must have a credible reason to be suspected (hidden motive, alibi gap, incriminating item, or cover-up).
   - **Single-Culprit Rule**: At most **1 player character** should have full prior knowledge of executing the central incident (never make 2+ players co-conspirators who already know the entire truth from page 1, as that eliminates investigative gameplay for too much of the table).
   - **Culprit Unknowns**: Even the responsible player MUST have at least **2 genuine unknowns** (e.g., who actually sent an anonymous warning, which piece of evidence survived, or what secret another player is hiding) so they must actively investigate and negotiate rather than merely playing passive defense.

3. **Conflicting & Overlapping Personal Objectives**
   - Mix objective archetypes across the cast: *Truth-seeker*, *Protector*, *Self-preservation*, *Opportunist / Retriever*, and *Reconciler / Persuader*.
   - Ensure at least two pairs of players have **directly competing** objectives, and at least two pairs have **potentially cooperative** objectives.

4. **Staged Revelations Across 3 Rounds**
   - **Round 1 (Introduction & Surface Alibis)**: Establish public identities, baseline timelines, and initial anomalies.
   - **Round 2 (Escalation & Secret Pressures)**: Release clues or trigger an event that breaks early assumptions, forcing private negotiations, clue trading, and alliances.
   - **Round 3 (Confrontation & Final Decision)**: Release decisive evidence that reframes an earlier clue (a fair twist supported by prior facts), leading to accusation, defense, and final voting/choices.

5. **Intentional Deception vs. Accidental Contradiction**
   - Every deliberate lie MUST be registered in the Information-Boundary Matrix as `[DELIBERATE LIE]` with: (a) what really happened, (b) what the character claims publicly, (c) why they lie, and (d) which clue exposes the lie.
   - Every red herring MUST be physically compatible with the Canonical Location Matrix and Timeline — misleading in *interpretation*, never physically impossible.

6. **Concrete Tone Execution**
   - Never merely declare `tone` in the header and write generic mystery prose.
   - Express the requested `tone` directly inside **Clue Player-Facing Content** and **Character Booklets** (e.g., for *comic/satire*, include absurd bureaucratic forms, petty grievances, and dryly funny prompts; for *noir*, use stark, cynical phrasing and high-stakes paranoia; for *melancholic/emotional*, highlight personal regret and quiet confessions).

## 3. Multiple Endings With a Fixed Central Truth

- **Fixed Truth Principle**: The core facts (who did what, when, how, and why) NEVER change across endings.
- **Branching Consequences**: Endings diverge based on observable player actions: final vote/accusation, secret exposure vs. cover-up, alliances, or crisis decisions.
- **Deterministic DM Adjudication**: Every ending must have an `IF ... THEN ...` trigger rule covering all plausible outcomes cleanly (no gaps or ambiguous overlaps), and every character's Primary/Secondary objective must have an objective `ACHIEVED IF ...` pass condition.
